1 · Definitions

Five words for talking about paper shapes

Plane

A perfectly flat surface that goes on forever in every direction. It has length and width but no thickness.

A tabletop is a small piece of a plane.

Surface

A two-dimensional object that can bend and curve in space: the skin of a ball, a sheet of paper, a paper loop.

Zoom in on any small patch and it looks like a piece of plane.

Side

A region you can travel all over without crossing an edge. A sheet of paper has two sides: front and back.

In a polygon, “side” means a line segment. Today it means a side of a surface.

Edge

A line where a surface stops. On a solid such as a cube, it is also a line where two faces meet.

A rectangle of paper has four edges.

Boundary

All of a surface's edges taken together. The boundary of a paper loop is two circles. The skin of a ball has no boundary.

Count boundary pieces: each separate closed loop of edge is one piece.

4 · Predict, then test

Write your answer down before you reveal it

1

How many sides and edges does a Möbius strip have?

Run a finger along the edge without lifting it. Where do you end up?

One side and one edge.

Your finger travels both long edges of the original strip, 56 cm in all, before it gets back to the start.

2

What happens when it is cut along the centre line?

Does it fall into two pieces like the ordinary loop?

It stays in one piece: a single loop twice as long (56 cm) and half as wide (1.5 cm), with two full twists.

The new loop has two sides and two edges, so it is no longer a Möbius strip.

3

What if you cut one third of the way in from the edge instead?

How many times do the scissors go round before they meet the start?

Two loops, linked together. The scissors go round twice. Out come a thin Möbius strip (28 cm × 1 cm) and a long two-sided loop (56 cm × 1 cm) with two full twists.

5 · Orientable and non-orientable

Can clockwise stay clockwise everywhere?

A surface is orientable if you can agree on one meaning of “clockwise” all over it. Slide a small letter F anywhere along the surface and it always comes back the right way round.

A surface is non-orientable if some path brings the F back as its mirror image. The Möbius strip is the simplest example.

Orientable

  • plane
  • sphere (skin of a ball)
  • ordinary loop, cylinder
  • torus (ring doughnut)

Non-orientable

  • Möbius strip
  • Klein bottle
  • projective plane

Any surface that contains a Möbius strip is non-orientable. For surfaces sitting in ordinary 3D space, orientable and two-sided mean the same thing.

Same net, different glue

Both nets are a 28 cm × 3 cm rectangle, so both surfaces have area 28 × 3 = 84 cm². The half-twist changes how the ends are joined, not the area.

Spatial reasoning

  • 2D → 3D: six squares joined edge to edge make 35 different shapes. Which fold into a cube? (11 of them do.)
  • 3D → 2D: the half-twist cannot be made on a flat table; you have to lift the paper into the third dimension.
  • Count: faces, edges and vertices of a cube satisfy V − E + F = 8 − 12 + 6 = 2.
